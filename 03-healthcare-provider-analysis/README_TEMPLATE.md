## The question

What were you actually trying to answer, and for whom? One short paragraph.
If the honest answer is "it was a course exercise", say so — a stated
constraint reads better than a pretended client.

## The data

Where it came from, how many rows, what period, and what is wrong with it.
Every real dataset has something wrong with it; saying what builds more
confidence than a clean description does.

## What I did

Short. The method, not the clicks. Nobody needs to read "I used Power Query to
import the CSV". They do want to know why you grouped it the way you did.

## What I found

Two or three charts, each one making a single point, each with a sentence
underneath saying what to look at. If a chart needs a paragraph to explain, it
is the wrong chart.

## What I would do differently

The section that separates a portfolio from a homework folder. Be specific:
a confounder you could not control for, a sample too small to support the cut
you made, a metric you would define differently now. One honest limitation is
worth more than three more charts.
